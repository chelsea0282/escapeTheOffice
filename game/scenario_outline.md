# Scenario Outline

## Scene 1: The Last Minute
The game opens at 4:47 PM. The player is preparing to leave and thinking about the concert, train, or evening plans they want to keep.

The first interruption is small and believable: a coworker or manager asks for a quick reply or a short favor.

## Scene 2: The Justification Loop
The player attempts to leave, defer, or refuse. The office responds by asking for a reason.

At this point the system begins to behave less like a human office and more like an evaluator: it wants to understand the player's priorities and whether their choices make sense.

## Scene 3: The Hidden Rubric
The player is now operating under a subtle pressure: every explanation can be interpreted as evidence of work ethic, ownership, or commitment.

The player may succeed by being clear, efficient, and strategic. But the game should make it feel as if the office is scoring their behavior even if the rubric is never fully visible.

## Scene 4: The Exit
The player finally reaches the elevator, the train, or the doorway.

This is where the game should reveal the cost of their choices: either a successful exit, a compromise, or a lingering sense that the system has already judged them.

## Ending Possibilities
- The player leaves and the office is left with an unresolved impression.
- The player stays and becomes entangled in yet another request.
- The player receives a late message or evaluation that suggests their behavior has been recorded and interpreted.
